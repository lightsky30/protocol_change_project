Fill your cup — and your heart too.

scroll ↓

There is a water cooler in the undergraduate branch library, and it has a quiet rule that no one wrote down. You come, you fill your cup, and you leave. You do it alone. You do it in silence. It takes about five seconds, and no one looks at anyone.

During exam season the rule gets stronger. Tired students come on autopilot, fill water, and go back to studying. Everyone here is tired in the same way — but no one shows it, and no one shares it.

What if the place where you fill water became the place where you fill yourself?

I didn't get here on the first try. My first attempt was in the space under the stairs in the Creative Learning Building. The idea wasn't bad, but the place was. It is a spot people just walk past without paying much attention. There was no moment when they slowed down, so almost no one used it.

That failure told me exactly what I needed: a place where people already stop for a few seconds, with nothing in their hands. The cooler is that place. I don't have to make anyone pause — the cooler already does. So I didn't build a new space. I added one small thing on top of a habit that was already there.

What I taped to the wall is just a QR code. Open it, and a wall of small notes appears — each one a line of mood and a song someone left behind. You can read what strangers wrote. You can leave your own, and pick a song for the next tired person. No names, no login. Only quiet encouragement, piling up.

I thought about a sign, but a sign is read once and changes nothing. I thought about a paper guestbook, but it would feel exposed, and it needs supplies and cleaning. A QR code and a wall on your own phone fit the rule of the place: it lives inside the five-second pause, it stays private on your screen, and it grows over time instead of standing still. It adds to the habit without breaking the silence.

You are not the only tired person standing here.

I couldn't stand there and watch someone use it. But in a single day, three new notes appeared on the wall. So even though I never saw it happen, people did stop, and they did leave something behind. I wasn't there to see their faces — but I hope, for a second, it put a small smile on one of them.

I hope many more people find it, and add to it, until the exams are over.

The wall is open.
